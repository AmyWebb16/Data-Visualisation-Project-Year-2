1. Dataset Description
1.1 Dataset Overview
The dataset contains yearly CO2 emissions for 199 countries from 1950 to 2024. The dataset extracted from CO2
and Greenhouse Gas Emissions dataset published by Our World in Data.
1.2 Dataset Source
Dataset was obtained from https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/lucalullo/global-co2-emissions-by-country-1950-2024
1.3 Dataset Structure
The Dataset has over 14.9k records with columns that include:
Country – country name corresponding with iso code.
Year – observation year.
Iso code – code of country.
Co2 – total territorial Co2 emissions (MtCo2).
Co2 per capita – CO2 emissions per person.
Coal co2 – Co2 emissions from coal combustion.
Oil co2 – Co2 emissions from oil.
Gas co2 – Co2 emissions from natural gas combustion.
Cement co2 – Co2 emissions from cement production.
Consumption co2 – consumption-based emissions including international trade.

Coal is by far the dominant fuel type at nearly 400,000 million tons, more than oil and gas together. This helps

4.2 Chart 2: Stacked Bar Chart – Fuel Type by Top 5 Emitters (2022)
This graph is interesting as it shows the contrast in countries’ fuel choices. China’s bar is majority dominated by
coal exceeding all other countries emissions. The USA in comparison is more balanced with a mix of oil, gas and

This relates back to Big Idea as it shows we must have fuel specific, country-level goals and not vague pledges. It

4.3 Chart 3: Line Graph – CO2 Emission Over Time
The graph shows the overall course of emissions from 1990 to 2024. The most important observation is that the
trend is frequently increasing, there are two stand out dips. Around 2008 when there is a global economic crisis
and 2020 during the covid-19 pandemic, these are important as it shows these decreases are only temporary,

This shows us that CO2 emissions have dropped not due to climate policy but economic collapses or global
health crisis. This displays even after years of international agreements, conventions and pledges the trend has
not changed. It supports the Big Idea that nations are not doing enough to bend the curve in a sustainable way.

The investigation of global CO2 emissions data from 1990 to 2024 shows a consistent and worrying gap between
what the nations commitments and the action taken that the data reflects.
Through both exploratory and explanatory visualisations, many observations stand out. Coal remains the largest
